Thank you very much for your time and valuable feedback on our manuscript. We sincerely appreciate your thoughtful comments and suggestions, which have greatly contributed to improving the quality of our paper. Here is our reply.

 > 1. The author's motivation is not clearly articulated. Moreover, the experimental setup is problematic. In lines 36-43, the authors claim that attention-based explanations struggle to capture global patterns, making them unsuitable for detection tasks. Previous research, specifically "Not All Tokens Are Created Equal: Perplexity Attention Weighted Networks for AI Generated Text Detection," demonstrates that attention mechanisms can indeed capture comprehensive information. The authors' claim appears inconsistent with this established work, and they provide insufficient support for their assertion.

Thanks for providing this reference paper. In our paper, the global patterns refer to the word frequency or words relationship. The attention-based methods are easy for instance-level explanation but not designed for global patterns, such as LLMs' preference. Furthermore, we do not deny the ability of these methods, such as attention-based approaches, to obtain explanations. In addition, "Not All Tokens Are Created Equal" is a preprint paper and first appear in arXiv on 7 Jan 2025. In general, this paper is a concurrent paper. Besides, this reference paper only consider the attention for detection not for explanation extraction. Technically, all detection methods can capture information, but not all of them can provide evidence to distinguish the MGTs and HGTs. So in this case, we do not think our motivation and experimental setup is problematic. 

> 2. They fail to adequately explain why current methods lack explainability. From my perspective, detectGPT's approach of extracting log probabilities for each token generated by LLMs aligns more closely with the fundamental nature of AI-generated text. This probabilistic approach potentially offers greater explainability than the authors' method, as it directly addresses the token-level generation process that distinguishes machine from human text.

Thanks for pointing out the probabilistic approach. In this paper, one of our goal is to provide the explainable motifs between HGTs and MGTs, which is designed for the task, not for explaining the method decision.
    The probabilistic method is logical and reasonable, however, this paper is not designed or explain a specific model. Our goal is to provide human-recognizable motifs for this task rather than explaining the method or model itself. Using the token-level log probabilities for the detection is a good idea and easy for humans to understand, but there is a lack of explanation/evidence for why the probability distribution is. Our goal is to extract the explanation motifs/evidence that support these detections.

> 3. The experimental design has significant limitations. The authors only test in-domain detection performance rather than generalization capabilities, which is a fundamental limitation given that in-domain detection is a relatively simple problem.

Thanks for providing this question. In out paper, we provide cross-domain analysis in Appendix C, as we mentioned in section 6, on line 312.

> 4. As shown in Appendix B.1 Table 4, the training, validation, and test sets are from the same domain. This does not effectively evaluate the method's practical utility, as even basic RoBERTa-based detectors can achieve around 98% accuracy in such controlled settings.

In previous papers, such as BiScope, RAIDAR, they also use similar settings. In this paper, we report the AUROC as the metric, in general, AUROC is higher than Accuracy. Besides, RoBERTa in this paper, is trained on the HC3 dataset. So it is reasonable why RoBERTa is good. To address this concern, we also provide accuracy based comparison on XXXX. In addition, we provide other experiments on Claude3-generated text on the Yelp domain. The results shown on XXX. LM$^2$otifs still achieves comparable performance.

Thanks for the valuable question. In our paper, LM$^2$OTIFS is the main framework, LM$^2$OTIFS-W is used to discuss the necessary for edge weight. > 7. The explainability claims are questionable. The method focuses only on word-level detection, which may fail when humans and machines share similar vocabulary in professional or specialized texts.

Thanks for the comments. In our paper, the detection processing is based on the graph, which contains tokens and the connection between tokens. It is not correct that our method "focuses only on word-level detection". In addition, the explanations are contains word-level and phrase-level (the connection between words), which is provided in Figure 4. 
    
From our experiments, in some domains, such as medicine, finance, we believe the vocabulary is similar in MGTs and HGTs. LM$^2$OTIFS achieves good performance. Besides, LM$^2$OTIFS can capture the connection between tokens, the reason why PGM is used. 

Furthermore, "The authors' critique of methods like DetectGPT for lacking explainability is unconvincing." lacks support. In our analysis of experiments, we do not have some claim like that. For the explainability of log probability distributions, we answer this part in question 2.
